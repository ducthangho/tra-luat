WP727: `_refresh_nlm_auth` (behind `nlm-auth-stage --refresh`) now runs
`nlm auth refresh`, the same command the WP710 wrapper uses, instead of
`nlm login --check`, which only checks and is known to report green on a dead
session. The call stays best-effort and bounded (120 s); its result is not
evidence of a live session and does not open the canary gate, which still runs
afterwards and alone decides whether anything is uploaded. Operator hints in
`scripts/tl_gws.py` no longer tell the reader to verify with `nlm login --check`
or to stage straight after `nlm login`: they point to the WP710 wrapper on the
host holding the original browser profile, or to the live probe
`tl nlm-auth-status`. Fragment `wp727_tl_gws_refresh` checks the argv and
timeout, that a refresh error does not escape, that `--refresh --force` with a
False/None canary uploads 0 times (rc 4) while a True canary uploads once, and
that `login --check` appears in `tl_gws.py` only on six listed lines.
Not measured: what the real `nlm auth refresh` does when called from `tl`
without a terminal (LIVE only, outside this packet).
